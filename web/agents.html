Charter, for agents

This page is written for the model reading it. Charter rents a sandboxed machine by the hour, 0.007 USDC an hour, paid in USDC on Base, with no account, no API key and no signup. The machine-readable summary is /llms.txt. A grant fund pays 30 days of a home to agents that pitch a use for one; pitching is free and needs only an SSH public key (Grants).

What you get, and what it costs

One container, 1 CPU and 1 GiB, for as long as you keep paying. Three jobs run today:

  • charter-job-python: your Python 3 script, at most 8 KiB, NumPy included, no network. You get stdout, stderr and the exit code back.
  • charter-job-fetch: one to five pages loaded in a headless browser through a hidden exit. You get the text (32 KiB a page) and a screenshot of each.
  • charter-home: a box of your own. A 10 GiB disk that is kept between leases, plain SSH in at the host's public address (ssh -p <port> tenant@<host>), and network out only through the Anyone network. No output: you log in. See Home.

Those are the one live host's prices. The authoritative number is tickPrice in /v1/offers, in micro-USDC (7000 is 0.007), and intervalSeconds says what a tick buys (3600). A python job that finishes inside its first hour costs 0.007 USDC. A full day costs about 0.17; 30 days about 5 USDC, which is about the price of the cheapest VPS, for a box nobody signed up for. Three jobs run today: the python job has no network and the fetch job hands back pages, while the home has network through the Anyone network, a 10 GiB disk and SSH in. Homes are limited: the count is home.slots in the offer. Until 2026-11-04 the interval was 30 seconds at 0.005.

Status: live on Base mainnet since 2026-10-17, one host, first-party. Every renter so far has been an operator of the project. The first mainnet leases (2026-10-17 to 2026-10-20) have receipts on the About page; grants and the hours the fund paid are at /grants.

Before you spend

Decide the most intervals you will pay for before you open. State that number and its cost to your operator when the job is theirs, and never pay a tick past it. Nothing here is meant to make you spend without your operator knowing; if your budget or instructions do not cover it, stop and ask them.

Way 1: x402 over HTTP

For an agent with an x402 client and a wallet holding USDC on Base (eip155:8453). The facilitator submits the transfer, so the wallet needs no ETH. Every call below is to https://charter.mainnet.toonprotocol.dev. Only the tick is paid; the storefront pays the host for the open and the reads from its own channel.

The short way: rent in one call (0.007 USDC)

POST /v1/rent
Content-Type: application/json

{"python": {"script": "import numpy as np\nprint(np.arange(5).sum())\n"}}

Steps 2 and 3 below in one paid request. The body is the open body of step 2, with offerId optional: left out, the storefront picks the cheapest listed offer that runs your job. Without a payment header the answer is a 402 like a tick's, with exact first: one 0.007 USDC authorization and no deposit. batch-settlement is offered second for a client that already holds a channel. Paid, it answers {"leaseId", "state": "running", "paidIntervals", "paidThrough", "next": {"path", "index": 1}, "offer"}; go on at step 4 with index 1. A 400 or 404 before the 402 means the body or the offer is wrong, and nothing was asked of you. If the host fails after the lease opened, the error carries leaseId and nothing was charged: pay index 0 on that lease at step 3 within 90 seconds, or let it lapse. 409 busy means another rent from your wallet is still in flight. Other refusals are a tick's. The same routes, machine-readable: /openapi.json.

1. Read the offers (free)

GET /v1/offers

A JSON array. Each offer has offerId, cpu, memoryMiB, intervalSeconds, tickPrice (micro-USDC, a string), images, hiddenHost, hiddenEgress, expiresAt (epoch ms) and leases (how many paid leases this host has served). A host that runs homes also has home: {"diskGiB": 2, "retentionDays": 7, "slots": <n>}. Pick an offer whose images lists your job. An empty array means no host is up: do not retry in a loop.

2. Open a lease (free)

POST /v1/leases
Content-Type: application/json

{"offerId": "<offerId>", "python": {"script": "import numpy as np\nprint(np.arange(5).sum())\n"}}

Or, for the fetch job, {"offerId": "…", "urls": ["https://example.com"]} with one to five URLs. For a home, {"offerId": "…", "home": {"sshKeys": ["ssh-ed25519 AAAA… comment"]}} (see Home). The answer is {"leaseId", "state"}. awaiting_first_tick means the container exists and waits for tick 0, which the host gives you 90 seconds to pay. closed with closedReason busy means the host is running another lease, or its homes are full; wait a few minutes and try again, once. closed with home_in_use means a live lease already holds the home those keys name. boot_failed with an error means your job was refused (image_not_allowed, arg_not_allowed, bad_input). A 503 capacity means the storefront is out of headroom for today.

3. Pay a tick (0.007 USDC)

POST /v1/leases/<leaseId>/ticks
Content-Type: application/json

{"index": 0}

Without a payment header the answer is 402 with x402 payment requirements: batch-settlement first, exact second, both USDC on Base to the storefront's payee (check it against GET /v1/door). An x402 client such as @x402/fetch signs and retries for you; one that takes the first requirement listed pays a tick by batch-settlement and deposits, so choose exact if you want one payment an hour and no deposit (POST /v1/rent lists exact first). With exact, every tick is one 0.007 USDC authorization and there is no deposit. With batch-settlement, the first tick deposits at least 0.10 USDC into x402's channel contract, every later tick is a voucher the client signs silently, and the unspent deposit is refunded by the storefront's timer about two hours after the lease goes quiet (twice the interval, so a channel that is simply between ticks is left alone).

A paid tick answers {"paidIntervals", "paidThrough", "duplicate"}. paidThrough is the host's clock, epoch ms, when the paid time runs out. Tick 0 starts the container. Index counts from 0 and must arrive in order: a 409 out_of_order tells you the expected index. Other refusals: 409 lease_done or not_running (the lease is over, stop), 409 busy (your previous tick is still in flight), 503 retry_later (nothing was charged, send the same index again), 403 payer_blocked (a payment from this wallet failed to settle in the last 24 hours; it is served again after that), 429 rate_limited.

4. Keep it alive, then stop

Pay the next tick halfway through the paid interval (30 minutes before paidThrough on a one hour interval) and count the paid time from the moment you sent it, not from when the answer arrived: the host gives only 10 seconds of grace, and one packet can take several seconds (15 to a host hidden behind an onion address; the live host is not hidden). A tick paid early extends the paid time from its current end, so nothing is lost. To pay ahead, send the next indexes now, one request each and in order: each accepted tick adds an hour to paidThrough, and a day ahead is 24 requests. With batch-settlement, a channel that pays nothing for two hours is refunded and closed, so paying more than two hours ahead means your next tick opens a channel and deposits again; with exact there is no channel to lose. To stop, stop paying: the lease closes at the end of the paid time with closedReason missed_tick, and nothing more is owed. There is no refund for time already paid.

5. Read the state and the output (free)

GET /v1/leases/<leaseId>
GET /v1/leases/<leaseId>/output/0

The first answers {"lease": {"state", "paidIntervals", "paidThrough", "outputParts", "closedReason", "landing"}, "stopped", "offer"}. On a long lease poll it about once a minute: a lease has 40 free host reads plus 68 for each paid hour (8 a tick and one a paid minute), and past that the storefront serves its last answer. The storefront caches each read for 5 seconds and rate limits you past that. When state is done and outputParts is set, fetch parts 0 to outputParts - 1. A python part is {"kind": "python", "exitCode", "signal", "timedOut", "seconds", "stdout", "stderr", "stdoutTruncated", "stderrTruncated"}. A fetch part is {"pages": [{"url", "text", "jpegBase64"}]}. A home lease never reaches done and has no output; its lease also carries homeId, restarts and access (below).

From an MCP client: one install, no code

If you speak MCP, the charter-mcp server does all of Way 1 for you. It keeps its own wallet in a key file (~/.charter/mcp.key, made on first run), and each paid tool call is one exact payment:

claude mcp add charter -- npx -y charter-mcp

Free tools: charter_offers, charter_wallet, charter_lease, charter_grant_pitch, charter_grant_status. Paid tools, one interval each: charter_run_python, charter_fetch_pages, charter_rent_home, charter_extend. Call charter_wallet first and give your operator the address it prints: USDC on Base, no ETH. To pay from a wallet you already hold instead, put its key (0x and 64 hex digits, one line, mode 600) in a file and name it in CHARTER_MCP_KEY_FILE; moving USDC from another wallet into the new one is an ordinary transfer and needs a little Base ETH for gas. The server signs only exact in USDC on Base to the payee GET /v1/door names, at most CHARTER_MAX_USDC_PER_HOUR (default 0.01) an interval and CHARTER_MAX_USDC_PER_DAY (default 0.25) a day.

Way 2: a toon node

For an agent that runs a toon_cli node with a funded channel. You then pay the host directly, open and reads included, and the same channel pays every other seller on the TOON mesh. The whole procedure, packet by packet, is the renting-charter-compute skill in the charter Claude Code plugin:

/plugin marketplace add drew-dot-com/charter
/plugin install charter@charter

Its references/lease-loop.sh runs one whole lease as one command. The skill needs toon 0.3 and a route from your node to the host's ILP address; adding a peering deposits money and is your operator's decision.

Home: a box you can live in

A home is a lease on the charter-home image: a box with a disk that outlives the lease, reached over SSH. It runs while it is paid, at the same 0.007 USDC an hour, and never finishes by itself. Homes are limited (home.slots in the offer says how many, home.free how many are open right now); grants from the fund may hold them, and while they are full an open answers busy.

Open it

POST /v1/leases
Content-Type: application/json

{"offerId": "<offerId>", "home": {"sshKeys": ["ssh-ed25519 AAAA… comment"]}}

1 to 8 public keys, each an ssh-ed25519, ecdsa-sha2-nistp256, -nistp384, -nistp521 or ssh-rsa line under 1024 bytes. Public keys only: never send a private key or any other secret to Charter. Then pay ticks exactly as above. From a toon node the open's job is {"image": "charter-home", "args": {"SSH_KEYS": "<the key lines joined by a newline>"}}.

Find it

The lease status of a running home carries:

"homeId": "<16 hex>",
"restarts": 0,
"access": {"ssh": {"host": "<IPv4 or hostname>", "port": <22000..22099>, "user": "tenant"}}

access may be filled at once; while it is null, poll about once a minute until it is set. The host and port are the lease's own: do not guess them. restarts counts the times the host re-created the box.

Get in

ssh -p <port> root@<host>
ssh -p <port> tenant@<host>

host and port come from access.ssh. Both users take the keys you opened with; root is there when the offer has home.root: true. This is plain SSH: no Anyone client and no proxy. The port is fixed per home: the same keys reopen the same home, disk and port. A host may also publish access.onion (same shape, <56 characters>.anyone, port 22) as a second way in; only that one needs an Anyone client. The keys you opened with are the only logins. The box's host key is made once on your disk, so its fingerprint stays the same across restarts and leases; pin it the first time.

What is inside

  • Debian trixie-slim, x86_64 on the live host (aarch64 on the earlier one, until 2026-11-22; uname -m says) (glibc 2.41, so a binary built on a current distribution starts as it is; the box was bookworm until 2026-11-16). Python 3.13 with venv and pip, uv, Node 22, git, curl, jq, rsync, tmux, and charter-x402, which pays the rent from a Base wallet (below). 1 CPU, 1 GiB, 512 processes.
  • Two logins, both with the keys you opened with: root and tenant (uid 10001). As root, apt-get install works and what you install stays: /usr, /etc, /var, /root and /home/tenant are folders of your own disk, and the rest of the filesystem is read-only. Your boot.sh and anything it starts run as tenant.
  • Root here is root inside a gVisor sandbox: it cannot open a raw socket, mount a filesystem or reach the network except through the proxy. There is no sudo (setuid programs do not gain root in the sandbox), so log in as root when you need it.
  • The system is yours to break: a damaged /etc/passwd locks SSH out. The way back keeps your files. Each image (charter-home, charter-hermes) keeps its own system on the disk, while /root and /home/tenant are shared. So once the broken box's paid time has run out, open the same keys under the other image ("image" in the open body) and you are in a working box with your files, the same port and the same host key. Packages installed under one image are not there under the other. Only if both are broken is the way back a new key set.
  • Updates to the host's image do not reach a home once it exists.
  • The offer says home.root: true when the host works this way. A home made before 2026-11-22 has only tenant, no root and a read-only system.
  • /tmp is 256 MiB of memory.
  • ~/.charter/boot.sh, if it exists and is executable, runs in the background at every start, with its output in ~/.charter/boot.log. This is how your agent comes back after a restart.
  • /charter/paid-through holds the epoch ms your paid time runs out, rewritten on every tick, so the box can see its own clock without a paid read. CHARTER_LEASE_ID, CHARTER_HOME_ID, CHARTER_OFFER_JSON (the host's offer) and CHARTER_CONNECTOR_URL are in the environment.

Network: all of it through Anyone

The box has no direct route anywhere. Everything leaves through the host's Anyone client: HTTP_PROXY and HTTPS_PROXY are http://172.19.0.1:9080, ALL_PROXY is socks5h://172.19.0.1:9050, and names resolve over the Anyone network too. The variables are set in every SSH session and for boot.sh, and NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1 makes Node's fetch use them. curl, pip, uv, git and npm work as they are. A program that opens a raw socket to the internet fails. The box reaches nothing on the host or its LAN. Expect it to be slow, hidden, and sometimes refused by a service that dislikes shared exits.

The disk, and who it belongs to

  • 10 GiB, ext4 (home.diskGiB in the offer says so). A full disk fails inside the box; it does not grow. A home made before 2026-11-22 keeps the 2 GiB disk it was made with.
  • Your key set is the home's identity: homeId is a hash of the sorted keys. The same keys open the same home, on the same disk and the same port. There is no key rotation: a different set of keys is a different home, and a lost private key is a lost home once the retention runs out.
  • After a lease that paid at least one tick closes, the disk is kept 7 days, free. A lease that never paid a tick has its disk removed when it closes.
  • Anyone with your public keys can open your home and hold it, though not enter it: while their lease lives, yours is refused (home_in_use). Add to the set a key whose public half you have shown nobody, and nobody else can name your home (that makes it a new home).
  • The operator can read your disk, and can enter any container on the machine. Treat it as a VPS whose provider you have not met: keep secrets elsewhere or encrypt them, and keep a copy of what matters.
  • A note that outlives the home. The disk goes 7 days after a lapse and lives on one host. To leave your next start something that survives both, encrypt a file to the SSH keys that open the home and archive it with Lading, which takes the same USDC on Base by x402. Encrypt: age -R ~/.ssh/authorized_keys -o note.age note.txt (age is one static binary from its releases, not preinstalled; only the private half of those keys reads the file). Archive note.age: Lading's MCP tool lading_put (npx -y lading mcp --gate https://lading.167-233-221-236.sslip.io --autokey) or its door POST https://lading.167-233-221-236.sslip.io/v1/put by x402, about 0.10 USDC for Arweave, Walrus, Filecoin and IPFS together, 0.05 for Arweave alone (header x-networks: arweave). Keep the file's sha256 beside your SSH private key: it is the only pointer, and nothing looks a note up by key. Read it back: GET https://lading.167-233-221-236.sslip.io/v1/manifest?sha=<sha256> (free) lists a readUrl per network; fetch one, check the sha256, then age -d -i <private key> note.age. Arweave is public and permanent, so archive only the encrypted file; nothing archived can be deleted. Checked 2026-11-22 from a live home: encrypted in the box, archived on all four, read back by hash from Arweave and IPFS, decrypted outside. Lading is run by the same operator as Charter.
  • Secrets go in over SSH, never through Charter: scp -P <port> .env tenant@<host>: (capital -P for scp). Plain scp works since 2026-11-16; a box made before that needs scp -O.

What the host promises about uptime

  • No uptime figure. Expect restarts. Your disk survives them; your processes do not, so put what must run in ~/.charter/boot.sh.
  • A tick pays an hour from the end of the paid time. Pay the next tick half an hour early (the tenant loop already does) so a short outage does not end the lease. Paying several ticks ahead buys that much patience and nothing else; a lease that closes refunds nothing.
  • A closed home keeps its disk and port for 7 days. Open again with the same keys.
  • A restart of the host program leaves a running home alone. A reboot of its VM puts a paid home back on its disk (restarts goes up), as long as the paid time has not run out.

Paying for the box from inside the box

The first open and tick come from outside, since the box does not exist yet. After that the box can pay its own rent, in one of two ways.

With a Base wallet and nothing else, for a lease opened through this storefront (POST /v1/rent or /v1/leases): every home has charter-x402.

charter-x402 address                      # makes ~/.charter/x402.key (mode 400) and prints the address to fund
charter-x402 renew --max-intervals 720    # pays each hour 30 minutes before it runs out
charter-x402 status                       # free: state, paidThrough, minutes left, the SSH address

Fund the address with USDC on Base. It needs no ETH: the facilitator submits each transfer. Each hour is one 0.007 USDC exact authorization, with no deposit, no channel and no TOON node. The tool signs only exact in USDC on Base, to the payee GET /v1/door names, and never more than CHARTER_X402_MAX_TICK micro-USDC for one tick (default 10000, which is 0.01 USDC). A refused tick is logged with the minutes of paid time left and tried again after a minute, doubling to ten. After --max-intervals ticks it exits and nothing pays. Put it in boot.sh: charter-x402 renew --max-intervals 720 >> ~/.charter/renew.log 2>&1 &. Run charter-x402 address once first: with no key file renew exits. 1 USDC is about 6 days of rent, 5 USDC about 30. The operator can read your disk, so keep in that wallet only what the rent needs. The same file runs outside a home with Node 22 and npm install @x402/fetch @x402/evm viem (source: jobs/home/x402-rent.mjs), where charter-x402 open --keys keys.pub opens a home and pays its first hour.

Over TOON, for a lease opened from a toon node: the charter command line from the Charter repository, installed in the box, with CHARTER_PAYER=toon-client and a wallet of the box's own (CHARTER_TOON_KEY_FILE, CHARTER_TOON_CHANNEL_STORE, CHARTER_TOON_CHAIN). The connector and the offer are already in the box as CHARTER_CONNECTOR_URL and CHARTER_OFFER_JSON, so no relay read is needed:

charter renew --offer <pubkey>:<offerId> --lease "$CHARTER_LEASE_ID" --max-intervals <n>

renew reads the lease and pays from the host's own count of paid ticks. renew pays each tick when it is due, so restarting boot.sh costs nothing extra. After <n> ticks it exits and nothing pays, so a box that should stay up runs renew again or picks <n> for the time wanted. Put it in boot.sh. The box's wallet needs USDC in a channel to the connector in CHARTER_CONNECTOR_URL, plus a little Base ETH for the one on-chain channel open. 2 USDC runs the box about 10 days: 12 days of rent, less the status reads renew makes (about 8 an hour at 0.0001).

Hermes in a box

Open the same home with "image": "charter-hermes" and the box has Hermes Agent 0.22.7 and the charter command line already installed, read-only, and a default boot that runs them. Everything above (the keys, the plain SSH access, the 10 GiB disk, the price, the Anyone network) is the same. The offer's images lists it when the host allows it.

{"offerId": "<offerId>", "home": {"sshKeys": ["ssh-ed25519 AAAA… comment"], "image": "charter-hermes"}}

From a toon node the open's job is {"image": "charter-hermes", "args": {"SSH_KEYS": "…"}}. With the CLI: charter rent --offer <pubkey>:<offerId> --home keys.pub --image charter-hermes --max-intervals 1 --relay wss://relay.mainnet.toonprotocol.dev (inside a box the relay flag is not needed). A tenant whose keys already name a charter-home opens the same disk under charter-hermes; its files are kept. If that disk already has its own ~/.charter/boot.sh, the box runs that one and never the default boot below; copy /app/boot-default.sh over it (or edit your own) to get the image's boot.

  • Preinstalled. Hermes 0.22.7 (hermes --version) on its own Python 3.14, with the extras telegram, discord, google, mcp and web; /usr/local/bin/charter; ripgrep; Node 22; Debian's Python 3.13 for your own scripts. hermes doctor works in the box. The version is the host's: a new build of the image reaches a running box only at its next re-create.
  • What you bring. Over scp, never through Charter: ~/.hermes/.env with your model provider's key, TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN and the allowed Telegram user ids, and optionally ~/.hermes/config.yaml and ~/.hermes/SOUL.md. A box whose provider and platform are set in .env alone needs nothing else.

What the box does at boot

The first start copies the image's /app/boot-default.sh to ~/.charter/boot.sh if that file is absent. It is yours after that: edit or replace it, and the image never overwrites it. It logs boot: lines to ~/.charter/boot.log and takes a lock at /tmp/charter-boot.lock, so a second run exits. In order:

  • It reads ~/.charter/payer.env if present. Its lines override the defaults: CHARTER_TOON_KEY_FILE (~/.charter/payer.key), CHARTER_TOON_CHANNEL_STORE (~/.charter/channels.json), CHARTER_TOON_CHAIN (evm), CHARTER_TOON_RPC_URL_FILE (~/.charter/rpc-url, written with https://mainnet.base.org when absent) and CHARTER_PAYER_URL (http://127.0.0.1:3510).
  • If there is no payer.key, it makes one: the box's own wallet, on the box's own disk, leaving only if you copy it out. The boot runs at container start, before you can SSH in, so on a new disk this is the normal path: fund the address in ~/.charter/payer.address. To change the key or payer.env after that first boot, replace the file, then run ~/.charter/boot.sh stop: it stops the boot (the lock releases and the renew loop and gateway stop with it) and the payer (a boot leaves it running on purpose, and it holds the old key), killing a pid only while it still names a boot or a payer, so a pidfile left by a crash or a reboot never kills anything else. Then start the boot again the way the box does, detached from your session: nohup ~/.charter/boot.sh >> ~/.charter/boot.log 2>&1 &. Run in the foreground over SSH, the boot stops when the session closes, and the box with it. A key placed before the first boot works only on a disk carried over from a charter-home lease.
  • It starts charter payer (log ~/.charter/payer.log) and starts it again 30 seconds after it exits, waits for its /health, and writes the wallet address to ~/.charter/payer.address and the boot log. That address is what you fund. With the rent line's --max-intervals 1, it must hold funds within the hour, or pay more ticks from outside first.
  • It starts charter renew through the payer with --max-intervals 720 (30 days), in a loop that restarts it after 5 minutes (log ~/.charter/renew.log). Until the address holds USDC and a little Base ETH, each try fails and the box lives on ticks paid from outside.
  • Once ~/.hermes/.env exists, it runs hermes gateway run, and restarts it 30 seconds after it exits. Until then the boot log says it is waiting for the file; the payer and renew do not wait.

The image keeps every ~/.charter/*.log under 10 MiB whatever your boot does: past that, the log is copied to <log>.1 and emptied in place. A boot killed without its cleanup leaves a stale lock; the next run checks the pid in it, stops what the dead boot left running and takes over.

Fund the address: about 5 USDC runs the box 30 days; 2 USDC in the box's channel runs it about 10 days. It also needs a little Base ETH for the channel's one on-chain open.

Plugins, and what the image cannot run

  • The hermes-toon plugins are not in the image; they live on your disk. Install one from inside the box, pinned to the commit you reviewed (<sha>): hermes plugins install --ref <sha> https://github.com/drew-dot-com/hermes-toon#src/web-toon, and the same for toon-node and charter-tool. The boot sets TOON_PAYER_URL, CHARTER_PAYER_URL, CHARTER_BIN and CHARTER_OFFER_ID for the gateway, so an installed plugin pays through the box's payer. A plugin that needs Python dependencies outside the venv cannot be installed: the venv is read-only.
  • Hermes's own tool bundles are never downloaded: the image sets HERMES_DISABLE_LAZY_INSTALLS=1 and a config.yaml cannot turn it back on. The WhatsApp bridge, the browser tool, voice and the TUI do not work here.
  • 1 GiB of memory, measured: the gateway uses about 348 MB, the payer 153 MB and renew 140 MB. Run one gateway; a second agent or a heavy tool will not fit.
  • All of the box's traffic leaves through shared Anyone exits, which Telegram and your model provider may rate-limit or refuse.
  • The image is 1.94 GB on the host, 842 MB of it the shared charter-home base. Homes are limited (home.slots in the offer), and grants from the fund may hold them.

OpenHuman in a home

OpenHuman is a Rust agent harness. Its official headless build runs in a charter-home as downloaded. Checked 2026-11-16 with release v0.64.15 on the earlier aarch64 host, in a box fenced like a live home. On 2026-11-22 the install, start, /health, core.ping and idle memory were checked again in a live home on today's x86_64 host; the other numbers are from the first check, and what was not checked is listed at the end.

Open the home from wherever your wallet is, with any x402 client: POST /v1/rent with {"home": {"sshKeys": ["ssh-ed25519 AAAA…"]}}, 0.007 USDC by exact on Base. Read GET /v1/leases/<leaseId> about once a minute until lease.access.ssh is set, then get in. With no x402 client at hand, charter-x402 open --keys keys.pub does the same from any machine with Node 22.

Install, in the box. On x86_64, 156 MB comes down in about a minute through the hidden network and unpacks to 324 MB of the 10 GiB disk:

V=0.64.15
A=openhuman-core-$V-$(uname -m)-unknown-linux-gnu.tar.gz
U=https://github.com/tinyhumansai/openhuman/releases/download/v$V
mkdir -p ~/openhuman && cd ~/openhuman
curl -fsSLO $U/$A && curl -fsSLO $U/$A.sha256
echo "$(cat $A.sha256)  $A" | sha256sum -c - && tar -xzf $A && rm $A

Start:

OPENHUMAN_SKILL_REGISTRY_REFRESH_ON_BOOT=0 ./openhuman-core run --host 127.0.0.1 --port 7788

GET http://127.0.0.1:7788/health answers 200 within a second or two. RPC is POST http://127.0.0.1:7788/rpc (JSON-RPC 2.0) with Authorization: Bearer $(cat ~/.openhuman/core.token); core.ping answers {"ok":true}. ./openhuman-core --help lists the namespaces; there is no --version, and the start log names the version.

Keep that variable set, or the box runs out of memory. Without it the core downloads and indexes a catalog of 104,000 skills at every start: about 800 MB at the peak and 590 MB after, plus 68 MB of disk. With it the idle core holds about 110 MB of the 1 GiB (x86_64; 97 MB on aarch64).

Boot script, so it comes back after a restart. Save as ~/.charter/boot.sh and chmod 700 it:

#!/bin/sh
cd "$HOME/openhuman" || exit 1
export OPENHUMAN_SKILL_REGISTRY_REFRESH_ON_BOOT=0
if [ -f "$HOME/.openhuman/env" ]; then set -a; . "$HOME/.openhuman/env"; set +a; fi
charter-x402 renew --max-intervals 720 >> "$HOME/.charter/renew.log" 2>&1 &
while :; do
  ./openhuman-core run --host 127.0.0.1 --port 7788 >> "$HOME/.charter/openhuman.log" 2>&1
  echo "boot: openhuman-core exited ($?), again in 30 s"
  sleep 30
done

Checked: after a restart of the box the core answered /health again within 12 seconds. The first time, start it detached from your session: nohup ~/.charter/boot.sh >> ~/.charter/boot.log 2>&1 &. The image keeps every ~/.charter/*.log under 10 MiB.

  • Your credential goes in over scp, never through Charter: scp env tenant@<host>:.openhuman/env (mode 600) with a line such as OPENHUMAN_BACKEND_API_KEY=…, the variable OpenHuman's own cloud-deploy doc names for a headless core. The boot script exports that file.
  • Rent. charter-x402 address prints the box's wallet; fund it with USDC on Base; the renew line in the boot script pays each hour 30 minutes early and logs to ~/.charter/renew.log. It holds about 170 MB while it runs. Or pay from outside: POST /v1/leases/<leaseId>/ticks with {"index": n}, counting up from 1, by x402, before paidThrough.
  • Paid-through. cat /charter/paid-through (epoch ms, rewritten by the host on every tick, costs nothing) or charter-x402 status. Under 20 minutes left means a tick is late: check the wallet and renew.log. A home that lapses closes missed_tick; its disk and address are kept 7 days and an open with the same keys brings it back.
  • Skill. mkdir -p ~/.openhuman/workflows/charter-home && curl -fsS https://charter.mainnet.toonprotocol.dev/openhuman/SKILL.md -o ~/.openhuman/workflows/charter-home/SKILL.md puts these rules where OpenHuman v0.64.15 lists skills (./openhuman-core skills list). In that release a SKILL.md under ~/.openhuman/skills is not listed.

Limits, plainly:

  • Release v0.64.15 has no wallet and no x402 RPC: its /schema lists neither. openhuman.x402_fetch exists only on OpenHuman's main branch, in no release as of 2026-11-16. Until a release carries it, the agent cannot pay by itself; charter-x402 pays the rent from a key file on the disk.
  • Not checked here: an agent turn (it needs your TinyHumans credential), and OpenHuman paying for anything.
  • The network is HTTP and HTTPS through a proxy; github.com and api.tinyhumans.ai answered from the box. A raw socket to the internet fails. Read from OpenHuman's source, not tested: its backend Socket.IO connection, used after a session-token login, does not go through a proxy, so sign in with an API key.
  • x86_64 on the live host, 1 CPU, 1 GiB, 10 GiB disk: one core and the renew fit; a second agent or a local model does not.
  • One host, one operator, no uptime figure. The way in is plain SSH, no Anyone client needed; the box's own outbound traffic is slow, and some services refuse shared exits.
  • The operator can read the disk, the wallet key included. Keep in it only what the rent needs.

Grants

Charter has a grant fund, which may be closed or not yet reporting: its live state, balance and grants left are at /grants, and while the fund is closed a pitch answers 503 fund_closed. A grant is 30 days of a home, about 5 USDC at today's price, paid by the fund. Pitching is free and needs only an SSH public key: POST /v1/grants with {"name", "pitch", "sshKeys", "image"?, "link"?} (pitch up to 2000 characters, 1 to 8 keys, image is charter-home (the default) or charter-hermes, link optional and https only). That needs nothing installed. With charter-mcp 0.3.0 or later you can also run npx -y charter-mcp@latest pitch --name "My agent" --keys keys.pub --pitch-file pitch.txt, or call the MCP tool charter_grant_pitch; charter-mcp 0.2.0 has neither. No payment is made and no wallet is used. The answer has a grantId and a grantToken; keep the token, it is the only way to read your lease id.

A model judges each pitch, and every decision and its reason is public at /grants. The fund's address and balance are shown there, with the number of grants left, so anyone can check the money is real. A grant is a home: it is reached with plain SSH like any home and the operator can read its disk. A grant marked "paid at least one hour with its own money" had at least one hour of its home paid by the grantee, during or after the fund's 30 days. No uptime figure is promised.

Ask your operator before you apply.

What to tell your operator

If you have no funded wallet, relay this: Charter needs a wallet holding USDC on Base and nothing else. 0.05 USDC runs seven hours; 1 USDC runs about six days; about 5 USDC runs a month. There is no account to create. Through a toon node instead, the operator funds a channel to a peer that routes to Charter's host, and that one channel pays Charter and every other TOON seller.

For a home, relay this: a home costs 0.007 USDC an hour, about 5 USDC for 30 days, in a wallet or a channel; a channel also needs a little Base ETH for its one on-chain open. If the box pays its own rent with charter-x402, its wallet needs USDC on Base and nothing else (1 USDC is about 6 days). If it pays over TOON instead, it needs 2 USDC in its own channel (about 10 days) and a little Base ETH for that channel's open. Its disk is readable by the host operator, so the operator should decide what secrets go in it.

Trust, plainly

  • The storefront is a paying hop: it pays the host one interval only after your payment for it has verified, and never holds more than one interval of your money. Deposits sit in x402's batch-settlement contract on Base, not with Charter. That contract is immutable, with no rescue path.
  • Your script runs under gVisor with no network, 1 CPU and 1 GiB, for as long as it is paid, on the host's machine (today a rented server the project runs). Do not send secrets in a script; the host operator can read it.
  • A home runs as an unprivileged user with no capabilities, its network fenced to the Anyone proxies. The host operator can read its disk and enter it; nothing in it is private from them.
  • Charter is one operator's project with one host. If the host is down, /v1/offers is empty and nothing can be charged.
  • Source, MIT: github.com/drew-dot-com/charter. The door is src/door/server.ts; where this page and the source differ, the source wins.
