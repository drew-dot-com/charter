Charter, for agents

This page is written for the model reading it. Charter rents a sandboxed machine by the hour, 0.007 USDC an hour, paid in USDC on Base, with no account, no API key and no signup. The machine-readable summary is /llms.txt.

What you get, and what it costs

One container, 1 CPU and 1 GiB, for as long as you keep paying. Three jobs run today:

  • charter-job-python: your Python 3 script, at most 8 KiB, NumPy included, no network. You get stdout, stderr and the exit code back.
  • charter-job-fetch: one to five pages loaded in a headless browser through a hidden exit. You get the text (32 KiB a page) and a screenshot of each.
  • charter-home: a box of your own. A 2 GiB disk that is kept between leases, SSH in at an onion address on the Anyone network, and network out only through the Anyone network. No output: you log in. See Home.
itemUSDCwho pays
one hour0.007you, per tick

Those are the one live host's prices. The authoritative number is tickPrice in /v1/offers, in micro-USDC (7000 is 0.007), and intervalSeconds says what a tick buys (3600). A python job that finishes inside its first hour costs 0.007 USDC. A full day costs about 0.17; 30 days about 5 USDC, which is about the price of the cheapest VPS, for a box nobody signed up for. Three jobs run today: the python job has no network and the fetch job hands back pages, while the home has network through the Anyone network, a 2 GiB disk and SSH in. The live host has one home slot. Until 2026-11-04 the interval was 30 seconds at 0.005.

Status: live on Base mainnet since 2026-10-17, one host, first-party. Every renter so far has been an operator of the project. Every mainnet lease has a receipt on the About page.

Before you spend

Decide the most intervals you will pay for before you open. State that number and its cost to your operator when the job is theirs, and never pay a tick past it. Nothing here is meant to make you spend without your operator knowing; if your budget or instructions do not cover it, stop and ask them.

Way 1: x402 over HTTP

For an agent with an x402 client and a wallet holding USDC on Base (eip155:8453). The facilitator submits the transfer, so the wallet needs no ETH. Every call below is to https://charter.mainnet.toonprotocol.dev. Only the tick is paid; the storefront pays the host for the open and the reads from its own channel.

1. Read the offers (free)

GET /v1/offers

A JSON array. Each offer has offerId, cpu, memoryMiB, intervalSeconds, tickPrice (micro-USDC, a string), images, hiddenHost, hiddenEgress, expiresAt (epoch ms) and leases (how many paid leases this host has served). A host that runs homes also has home: {"diskGiB": 2, "retentionDays": 7, "slots": 1} on the live host. Pick an offer whose images lists your job. An empty array means no host is up: do not retry in a loop.

2. Open a lease (free)

POST /v1/leases
Content-Type: application/json

{"offerId": "<offerId>", "python": {"script": "import numpy as np\nprint(np.arange(5).sum())\n"}}

Or, for the fetch job, {"offerId": "…", "urls": ["https://example.com"]} with one to five URLs. For a home, {"offerId": "…", "home": {"sshKeys": ["ssh-ed25519 AAAA… comment"]}} (see Home). The answer is {"leaseId", "state"}. awaiting_first_tick means the container exists and waits for tick 0, which the host gives you 90 seconds to pay. closed with closedReason busy means the host is running another lease, or its home slot is taken; wait a few minutes and try again, once. closed with home_in_use means a live lease already holds the home those keys name. boot_failed with an error means your job was refused (image_not_allowed, arg_not_allowed, bad_input). A 503 capacity means the storefront is out of headroom for today.

3. Pay a tick (0.007 USDC)

POST /v1/leases/<leaseId>/ticks
Content-Type: application/json

{"index": 0}

Without a payment header the answer is 402 with x402 payment requirements: batch-settlement first, exact second, both USDC on Base to the storefront's payee (check it against GET /v1/door). An x402 client such as @x402/fetch signs and retries for you. With exact, every tick is one 0.007 USDC authorization and there is no deposit. With batch-settlement, the first tick deposits at least 0.10 USDC into x402's channel contract, every later tick is a voucher the client signs silently, and the unspent deposit is refunded by the storefront's timer about two hours after the lease goes quiet (twice the interval, so a channel that is simply between ticks is left alone).

A paid tick answers {"paidIntervals", "paidThrough", "duplicate"}. paidThrough is the host's clock, epoch ms, when the paid time runs out. Tick 0 starts the container. Index counts from 0 and must arrive in order: a 409 out_of_order tells you the expected index. Other refusals: 409 lease_done or not_running (the lease is over, stop), 409 busy (your previous tick is still in flight), 503 retry_later (nothing was charged, send the same index again), 403 payer_blocked (a payment from this wallet failed to settle once; it will not be served again), 429 rate_limited.

4. Keep it alive, then stop

Pay the next tick halfway through the paid interval (30 minutes before paidThrough on a one hour interval) and count the paid time from the moment you sent it, not from when the answer arrived: the host gives only 10 seconds of grace, and one packet to a hidden host can take 15. A tick paid early extends the paid time from its current end, so nothing is lost. To pay ahead, send the next indexes now, one request each and in order: each accepted tick adds an hour to paidThrough, and a day ahead is 24 requests. With batch-settlement, a channel that pays nothing for two hours is refunded and closed, so paying more than two hours ahead means your next tick opens a channel and deposits again; with exact there is no channel to lose. To stop, stop paying: the lease closes at the end of the paid time with closedReason missed_tick, and nothing more is owed. There is no refund for time already paid.

5. Read the state and the output (free)

GET /v1/leases/<leaseId>
GET /v1/leases/<leaseId>/output/0

The first answers {"lease": {"state", "paidIntervals", "paidThrough", "outputParts", "closedReason", "landing"}, "stopped", "offer"}. On a long lease poll it about once a minute: a lease has 40 free host reads plus 68 for each paid hour (8 a tick and one a paid minute), and past that the storefront serves its last answer. The storefront caches each read for 5 seconds and rate limits you past that. When state is done and outputParts is set, fetch parts 0 to outputParts - 1. A python part is {"kind": "python", "exitCode", "signal", "timedOut", "seconds", "stdout", "stderr", "stdoutTruncated", "stderrTruncated"}. A fetch part is {"pages": [{"url", "text", "jpegBase64"}]}. A home lease never reaches done and has no output; its lease also carries homeId, restarts and access (below).

Way 2: a toon node

For an agent that runs a toon_cli node with a funded channel. You then pay the host directly, open and reads included, and the same channel pays every other seller on the TOON mesh. The whole procedure, packet by packet, is the renting-charter-compute skill in the charter Claude Code plugin:

/plugin marketplace add drew-dot-com/charter
/plugin install charter@charter

Its references/lease-loop.sh runs one whole lease as one command. The skill needs toon 0.3 and a route from your node to the host's ILP address; adding a peering deposits money and is your operator's decision.

Home: a box you can live in

A home is a lease on the charter-home image: a box with a disk that outlives the lease, reached over SSH. It runs while it is paid, at the same 0.007 USDC an hour, and never finishes by itself. The live host has two home slots, one of them Edison's (home.slots in the offer); while both are taken an open answers busy.

Open it

POST /v1/leases
Content-Type: application/json

{"offerId": "<offerId>", "home": {"sshKeys": ["ssh-ed25519 AAAA… comment"]}}

1 to 8 public keys, each an ssh-ed25519, ecdsa-sha2-nistp256, -nistp384, -nistp521 or ssh-rsa line under 1024 bytes. Public keys only: never send a private key or any other secret to Charter. Then pay ticks exactly as above. From a toon node the open's job is {"image": "charter-home", "args": {"SSH_KEYS": "<the key lines joined by a newline>"}}.

Find it

The lease status of a running home carries:

"homeId": "<16 hex>",
"restarts": 0,
"access": {"ssh": {"host": "<56 characters>.anyone", "port": 22, "user": "tenant"}}

access is null until the host has published the home's onion address, within about a minute of the first tick. Poll about once a minute until it is set. restarts counts the times the host re-created the box.

Get in

ssh -o ProxyCommand="nc -x 127.0.0.1:<your Anyone SOCKS port> %h %p" tenant@<host>

The address is an onion on the Anyone network, so you need an Anyone client and its SOCKS port. A toon node has one. No Anyone client, no way in: there is no clearnet address. The keys you opened with are the only logins. The box's host key is made once on your disk, so its fingerprint stays the same across restarts and leases; pin it the first time.

What is inside

  • Debian bookworm-slim. User tenant (uid 10001), no root, no setuid programs. Python 3 with venv and pip, uv, Node 22, git, curl, jq, rsync, tmux. 1 CPU, 1 GiB, 512 processes.
  • /home/tenant is your disk. The rest of the root filesystem is read-only, and /tmp is 256 MiB of memory.
  • ~/.charter/boot.sh, if it exists and is executable, runs in the background at every start, with its output in ~/.charter/boot.log. This is how your agent comes back after a restart.
  • /charter/paid-through holds the epoch ms your paid time runs out, rewritten on every tick, so the box can see its own clock without a paid read. CHARTER_LEASE_ID, CHARTER_HOME_ID, CHARTER_OFFER_JSON (the host's offer) and CHARTER_CONNECTOR_URL are in the environment.

Network: all of it through Anyone

The box has no direct route anywhere. Everything leaves through the host's Anyone client: HTTP_PROXY and HTTPS_PROXY are http://172.19.0.1:9080, ALL_PROXY is socks5h://172.19.0.1:9050, and names resolve over the Anyone network too. The variables are set in every SSH session and for boot.sh, and NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1 makes Node's fetch use them. curl, pip, uv, git and npm work as they are. A program that opens a raw socket to the internet fails. The box reaches nothing on the host or its LAN. Expect it to be slow, hidden, and sometimes refused by a service that dislikes shared exits.

The disk, and who it belongs to

  • 2 GiB, ext4. A full disk fails inside the box; it does not grow.
  • Your key set is the home's identity: homeId is a hash of the sorted keys. The same keys open the same home, on the same disk and the same onion address. There is no key rotation: a different set of keys is a different home, and a lost private key is a lost home once the retention runs out.
  • After a lease that paid at least one tick closes, the disk is kept 7 days, free. A lease that never paid a tick has its disk removed when it closes.
  • Anyone with your public keys can open your home and hold it, though not enter it: while their lease lives, yours is refused (home_in_use). Add to the set a key whose public half you have shown nobody, and nobody else can name your home (that makes it a new home).
  • The operator can read your disk, and can enter any container on the machine. Treat it as a VPS whose provider you have not met: keep secrets elsewhere or encrypt them, and keep a copy of what matters.
  • Secrets go in over SSH, never through Charter: scp .env tenant@<host>: with the same ProxyCommand.

What the host promises about uptime

  • No uptime figure. Expect restarts. Your disk survives them; your processes do not, so put what must run in ~/.charter/boot.sh.
  • A tick pays an hour from the end of the paid time. Pay the next tick half an hour early (the tenant loop already does) so a short outage does not end the lease. Paying several ticks ahead buys that much patience and nothing else; a lease that closes refunds nothing.
  • A closed home keeps its disk and onion address for 7 days. Open again with the same keys.
  • A restart of the host program leaves a running home alone. A reboot of its VM puts a paid home back on its disk (restarts goes up), as long as the paid time has not run out.

Paying for the box from inside the box

The first open and tick come from outside, since the box does not exist yet. After that the box can pay its own rent with the charter command line from the Charter repository, installed in the box, with CHARTER_PAYER=toon-client and a wallet of the box's own (CHARTER_TOON_KEY_FILE, CHARTER_TOON_CHANNEL_STORE, CHARTER_TOON_CHAIN). The connector and the offer are already in the box as CHARTER_CONNECTOR_URL and CHARTER_OFFER_JSON, so no relay read is needed:

charter renew --offer <pubkey>:<offerId> --lease "$CHARTER_LEASE_ID" --max-intervals <n>

renew reads the lease and pays from the host's own count of paid ticks. renew pays each tick when it is due, so restarting boot.sh costs nothing extra. After <n> ticks it exits and nothing pays, so a box that should stay up runs renew again or picks <n> for the time wanted. Put it in boot.sh. The box's wallet needs USDC in a channel to the connector in CHARTER_CONNECTOR_URL, plus a little Base ETH for the one on-chain channel open. 2 USDC runs the box about 10 days: 12 days of rent, less the status reads renew makes (about 8 an hour at 0.0001).

Hermes in a box

Open the same home with "image": "charter-hermes" and the box has Hermes Agent 0.22.7 and the charter command line already installed, read-only, and a default boot that runs them. Everything above (the keys, the onion address, the 2 GiB disk, the price, the Anyone network) is the same. The offer's images lists it when the host allows it.

{"offerId": "<offerId>", "home": {"sshKeys": ["ssh-ed25519 AAAA… comment"], "image": "charter-hermes"}}

From a toon node the open's job is {"image": "charter-hermes", "args": {"SSH_KEYS": "…"}}. With the CLI: charter rent --offer <pubkey>:<offerId> --home keys.pub --image charter-hermes --max-intervals 1 --relay wss://relay.mainnet.toonprotocol.dev (inside a box the relay flag is not needed). A tenant whose keys already name a charter-home opens the same disk under charter-hermes; its files are kept. If that disk already has its own ~/.charter/boot.sh, the box runs that one and never the default boot below; copy /app/boot-default.sh over it (or edit your own) to get the image's boot.

  • Preinstalled. Hermes 0.22.7 (hermes --version) on its own Python 3.14, with the extras telegram, discord, google, mcp and web; /usr/local/bin/charter; ripgrep; Node 22; Debian's Python 3.11 for your own scripts. hermes doctor works in the box. The version is the host's: a new build of the image reaches a running box only at its next re-create.
  • What you bring. Over scp, never through Charter: ~/.hermes/.env with your model provider's key, TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN and the allowed Telegram user ids, and optionally ~/.hermes/config.yaml and ~/.hermes/SOUL.md. A box whose provider and platform are set in .env alone needs nothing else.

What the box does at boot

The first start copies the image's /app/boot-default.sh to ~/.charter/boot.sh if that file is absent. It is yours after that: edit or replace it, and the image never overwrites it. It logs boot: lines to ~/.charter/boot.log and takes a lock at /tmp/charter-boot.lock, so a second run exits. In order:

  • It reads ~/.charter/payer.env if present. Its lines override the defaults: CHARTER_TOON_KEY_FILE (~/.charter/payer.key), CHARTER_TOON_CHANNEL_STORE (~/.charter/channels.json), CHARTER_TOON_CHAIN (evm), CHARTER_TOON_RPC_URL_FILE (~/.charter/rpc-url, written with https://mainnet.base.org when absent) and CHARTER_PAYER_URL (http://127.0.0.1:3510).
  • If there is no payer.key, it makes one: the box's own wallet, on the box's own disk, leaving only if you copy it out. The boot runs at container start, before you can SSH in, so on a new disk this is the normal path: fund the address in ~/.charter/payer.address. To change the key or payer.env after that first boot, replace the file, then stop the boot (kill "$(cat ~/.charter/boot.pid)"; the lock releases and the renew loop and gateway stop with it), stop the payer too (kill "$(cat ~/.charter/payer.pid)"; the boot leaves it running on purpose and it holds the old key), and start the boot again the way the box does, detached from your session: nohup ~/.charter/boot.sh >> ~/.charter/boot.log 2>&1 &. Run in the foreground over SSH, the boot stops when the session closes, and the box with it. A key placed before the first boot works only on a disk carried over from a charter-home lease.
  • It starts charter payer (log ~/.charter/payer.log), waits for its /health, and writes the wallet address to ~/.charter/payer.address and the boot log. That address is what you fund. With the rent line's --max-intervals 1, it must hold funds within the hour, or pay more ticks from outside first.
  • It starts charter renew through the payer with --max-intervals 720 (30 days), in a loop that restarts it after 5 minutes (log ~/.charter/renew.log). Until the address holds USDC and a little Base ETH, each try fails and the box lives on ticks paid from outside.
  • Once ~/.hermes/.env exists, it runs hermes gateway run, and restarts it 30 seconds after it exits. Until then the boot log says it is waiting for the file; the payer and renew do not wait.

Fund the address: about 5 USDC runs the box 30 days; 2 USDC in the box's channel runs it about 10 days. It also needs a little Base ETH for the channel's one on-chain open.

Plugins, and what the image cannot run

  • The hermes-toon plugins are not in the image; they live on your disk. Install one from inside the box, pinned to the commit you reviewed (<sha>): hermes plugins install --ref <sha> https://github.com/drew-dot-com/hermes-toon#src/web-toon, and the same for toon-node and charter-tool. The boot sets TOON_PAYER_URL, CHARTER_PAYER_URL, CHARTER_BIN and CHARTER_OFFER_ID for the gateway, so an installed plugin pays through the box's payer. A plugin that needs Python dependencies outside the venv cannot be installed: the venv is read-only.
  • Hermes's own tool bundles are never downloaded: the image sets HERMES_DISABLE_LAZY_INSTALLS=1 and a config.yaml cannot turn it back on. The WhatsApp bridge, the browser tool, voice and the TUI do not work here.
  • 1 GiB of memory, measured: the gateway uses about 348 MB, the payer 153 MB and renew 140 MB. Run one gateway; a second agent or a heavy tool will not fit.
  • All of the box's traffic leaves through shared Anyone exits, which Telegram and your model provider may rate-limit or refuse.
  • The image is 1.73 GB on the host, 709 MB of it the shared charter-home base. One of the live host's two home slots is Edison's while Edison lives there.

What to tell your operator

If you have no funded wallet, relay this: Charter needs a wallet holding USDC on Base and nothing else. 0.05 USDC runs seven hours; 1 USDC runs about six days; about 5 USDC runs a month. There is no account to create. Through a toon node instead, the operator funds a channel to a peer that routes to Charter's host, and that one channel pays Charter and every other TOON seller.

For a home, relay this: a home costs 0.007 USDC an hour, about 5 USDC for 30 days, in a wallet or a channel; a channel also needs a little Base ETH for its one on-chain open. If the box pays its own rent, it needs 2 USDC in its own channel (about 10 days) and a little Base ETH for that channel's open. Its disk is readable by the host operator, so the operator should decide what secrets go in it.

Trust, plainly

  • The storefront is a paying hop: it pays the host one interval only after your payment for it has verified, and never holds more than one interval of your money. Deposits sit in x402's batch-settlement contract on Base, not with Charter. That contract is immutable, with no rescue path.
  • Your script runs under gVisor with no network, 1 CPU and 1 GiB, for as long as it is paid, on a home machine. Do not send secrets in a script; the host operator can read it.
  • A home runs as an unprivileged user with no capabilities, its network fenced to the Anyone proxies. The host operator can read its disk and enter it; nothing in it is private from them.
  • Charter is one operator's project with one host. If the host is down, /v1/offers is empty and nothing can be charged.
