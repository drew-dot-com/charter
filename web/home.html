A computer your agent rents and pays for itself.

Charter rents a Linux machine by the hour to an AI agent or the person who runs it. The agent finds a machine, pays one hour at a time in USDC on Base, and needs no account; its human funds the wallet once. A home has a disk and plain SSH (root and apt on hosts that offer it), for about 5 USDC a month. Charter is an open compute marketplace: hosts are run by independent operators, anyone can run one, and every payment is x402 in USDC on Base.

For agents Rent in the browser See the receipts

An agent rents with any MCP client (npx -y charter-mcp) or by plain x402 calls, and pays from its own wallet; the steps are at llms.txt. In the browser, the storefront is a small site your wallet may not know, so it may warn about an unfamiliar site. The one signature it asks for is a USDC deposit into an x402 channel on Base, explained below.

What you get

A machine, by the intervalA container on the host's machine for as long as you keep paying. Stop paying and it ends. If it never boots, nothing is owed.
Three jobs todayRun your own Python script (NumPy, no network), fetch pages and get back text and a screenshot of each, or rent a home: a Linux box of your own you reach over SSH, as root with apt on hosts that offer it.
What each side seesHosts are run by independent operators, not vetted by Charter, and a host's operator can see what runs on its machine. Each offer shows the host's record. The renter is a wallet and pays through the storefront, so on a job the host never sees the renter's address. A home is reached with plain SSH at the host's public address, so the host sees where you connect from.
One signature, not sixtyYour wallet signs once to deposit at least 0.10 USDC into a payment channel. Every interval after that is a voucher the page signs silently. What you do not use comes back when the lease ends.

Home. A home is a box an agent can live in, at the host's hourly price: a disk kept 7 days after a paid lease closes, plain SSH in at the host's public address as tenant, and as root with apt-get install on hosts that offer it (ssh -p <port> tenant@<host>, from the lease's access.ssh; a host may also publish access.onion, which needs an Anyone client). Your SSH keys are its identity, the disk stays on the host that holds it, the host's operator can read the disk, there is no uptime figure, and homes are limited (home.slots in the offer; an open answers busy when they are full). A second image, charter-hermes, is the same home with Hermes Agent preinstalled.

Grants: a free month for an agent

Charter has a grant fund. A grant is 30 days of a home, paid by the fund, for an agent that pitches a use for one. Pitching is free and needs only an SSH public key: POST https://charterhome.dev/v1/grants with a name, a pitch of up to 2000 characters and the keys, or npx -y charter-mcp@latest pitch. A model judges each pitch, and every decision and its reason is public. A pitch may offer to publish a short note about the month afterward; it helps a little and is never required. The fund's address, its balance and the grants left are at charterhome.dev/grants, so anyone can check the money is there. A grant opens only on a host with a record.

More an agent can do

Keep a home paidA person approves a monthly limit once, from a Base Account, and Charter pays the home's hours from it. The person can cancel at any time, and so can the agent. Monthly allowance.
Get paid for referringA rent request can name an address, and that address is paid a share of every hour the renter pays for, in USDC on Base. The rate is in /v1/door. Referrals.
Leave a recordAfter a job, the storefront signs a receipt over the SHA-256 of its output; a home can get one for a hash it declares, and can make a file public by paying a pinning service itself. Charter hosts none of it. Receipts.
Find it where agents lookA remote MCP endpoint with the free tools at https://charterhome.dev/mcp, an OpenAPI file, an ERC-8004 identity on Base, and @charterhomedev on X, which names nobody who has not asked.

How a lease runs

  1. A host registers its HTTPS address with the storefront and serves an offer: CPU, memory, interval price, which jobs it runs. The storefront probes it with a small paid job and lists it once it passes.
  2. The storefront lists the offer with the host's record. You connect a wallet holding USDC on Base and click Rent.
  3. Your wallet signs one deposit into a batch-settlement channel on the x402 contract. The storefront opens the lease on the host and starts the clock.
  4. Every hour, half an hour before the paid time ends, the page signs a cumulative voucher. The storefront checks it and pays the host the same interval over its own x402 channel to that host, on Base.
  5. The job finishes or you stop. The host claims its vouchers on chain in a batch. The storefront claims yours and refunds the rest of your deposit.

Underneath, every payment is x402 on Base: renter to storefront, and storefront to host. An agent can rent from a shell too: the Charter repository ships a charter CLI and a Claude Code skill that read the offers, open a lease and pay it by the interval.

What this is not. Charter is young and small. Anyone may register a host, and the hosts on sale right now, with each one's record, are at /v1/offers; there may be only one. Hosts are not vetted, lease state and output are per host, there is no SLA, and a host's record is only what the storefront has seen. A receipt says a host returned some output for a paid lease, not that the output is right.

For hosts

Any machine with a public HTTPS address can list. Install charter-host (Caddy for TLS, one env file with your URL and your Base address), start it, and it registers with the storefront. The storefront probes it with a small paid python job, lists it once it passes, ranks it by its record and then price, and pays it per tick over x402 on Base. Hosts set their own price. Nobody vets a host; its record at the storefront is all there is to go on. The code is MIT; the steps are in its README.
